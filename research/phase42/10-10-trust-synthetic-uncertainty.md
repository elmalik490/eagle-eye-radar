# البيانات الاصطناعية والثقة والنسب وعدم اليقين

## Evidence note

**Scope and distinction.** Eagle Eye uses no AI today (product context supplied for this brief). That does not make deterministic rules, geocoding, or synthetic map points “verified intelligence.” The core product judgment is to expose *what kind of signal a record is* before showing an interpretation. NIST defines risk in terms of likelihood and consequence and says risk management should account for inherent model/system limitations and uncertainties; it also warns that people may assume automated systems work well in all settings [1]. This is a useful standard even for non-AI rules: a deterministic output can be reproducible without being true.

**Synthetic data is not proof.** The UK Office for National Statistics (ONS) says synthetic data are artificial data intended not to refer to real individuals, and that they may be useful for testing, research, and statistical purposes. It explicitly says synthetic data should be expected *not* to reflect all properties of real data, and that errors and differences should be expected [2]. UK Statistics Authority guidance adds that higher-fidelity synthesis can coincidentally reproduce real combinations, especially for small groups or sensitive variables such as location; synthetic data are therefore not risk-free [3]. The same guidance recommends that shared materials clearly label how representative the synthetic data are, and explain effects on quality and disclosure risk [3].

For Eagle Eye, “synthetic geography” should therefore be a first-class provenance state, not a visual style. A generated coordinate, interpolated boundary, centroid, heat cell, or scenario point may be useful for exploration, but it is a *derived representation* rather than an observed event. Do not place it in the same layer, legend, or default result count as source-backed observations. If a synthetic point is necessary, label it directly: **“SYNTHETIC / FOR SIMULATION — not an observed location.”** Add a tooltip: **“Generated from [method]; source record: none; geographic precision: modeled; do not use as confirmation.”**

**Signal versus proof.** C2PA is a relevant provenance model because it separates signed claims and assertions from conclusions. Its specification says assertions are “trust signals,” and that the standard validates whether assertions are associated with an asset, correctly formed, and untampered—not whether the underlying assertion is good or true [4]. It distinguishes *Well-Formed*, *Valid*, and *Trusted* manifest states; a valid signature establishes attribution to the claim generator, not truth of a GPS value or other asserted content [4]. This is the right mental model for a radar UI: provenance can increase traceability and integrity without upgrading a weak signal into verified intelligence.

Recommended evidence states are: **Observed** (direct source artifact available); **Reported** (a source asserts it, but Eagle Eye has not independently verified it); **Derived** (computed from observed/reported inputs); **Synthetic** (generated or simulated, with no real-world event implied); **Unresolved** (conflicting, incomplete, stale, or unverifiable); and **Verified** only when a documented verification procedure and source support exist. A separate confidence field should never collapse these states. Use wording such as **“Confidence: medium in the rule match; not confidence in the event’s truth.”** Avoid “confirmed,” “located,” or “intelligence” for a rule match unless the verification gate has actually passed.

**Provenance and uncertainty.** W3C PROV defines provenance as information about entities, activities, and people involved in producing data, used to assess quality, reliability, or trustworthiness, and supports representing processing steps, derivation, versioning, and reproducibility [5]. NIST’s Generative AI Profile similarly recommends documenting the origin and history of training and generated data, tracing origin and modifications, analyzing provenance, and documenting provenance limitations [6]. Eagle Eye can apply this without AI by storing: source URL/identifier; capture time; collector; transformation/rule name and version; input IDs; geometry method and precision; missingness/conflicts; and review status.

**Concrete UI safeguards (product judgment grounded in the above standards).** Put a persistent provenance badge beside every map item, not only in a details drawer. Example badge copy: **“DERIVED — 2 inputs; rule: geocode-v3; last run 14:32 UTC.”** For synthetic geography: **“SYNTHETIC — scenario geometry; no source observation.”** For a deterministic heuristic: **“RULE MATCH — repeatable pattern, not independent corroboration.”** For an unverified report: **“REPORTED — source claim; verification pending.”** The action button should say **“Open source and verify”**, not “Confirm.” In list and export views, prepend the state to the title and include a machine-readable `evidence_state` and `provenance` object. Disable “verified” filters for records lacking the verification state, and require a reviewer, method, timestamp, and cited source before promoting an item.
